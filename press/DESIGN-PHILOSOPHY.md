# Pencilled Light

*A visual philosophy for the Çoban press graphics.*

**Light first, then the hand.** Every composition begins with warm, low light: the long hour
before sunset, when the land turns apricot and every animal throws a shadow three times its
length. That light is never invented. It is collected, framed and treated with respect. On top
of it, and only where it earns the space, comes the hand: graphite marks, a ring of beads, a
compass notch, the thin line of a route. Light is the subject; the hand is the witness that
was there and wrote it down.

**The page as a field notebook.** Space works like a well-kept notebook: generous margins,
one clear thing per page, and small notes in the corners where a careful observer would put
them. Hierarchy is quiet and certain. There is one large gesture (a wordmark, a horizon, a
single animal), one middle voice, and a scatter of tiny reference marks: coordinates, hours,
counts, tick marks. Nothing shouts twice. Each placement should feel like something a master
draughtsman settled after many patient revisions.

**Colour from the ground up.** The palette is mineral and sun-warmed: terracotta roof tile,
ochre grass, olive and cypress green, dusk rose, lavender haze, the cream of old paper, and an
iron-gall brown for ink. Night is one deep indigo lit by a single amber flame. Colours are
never decorative. Each one is a material you could pick up in the valley, and they should sit
together with the calibrated calm of something worked over for weeks.

**Counting as a form of love.** Repetition carries the meaning. Twenty-four beads for
twenty-four sheep. Five spokes for five whistles. Contour after contour. Patterns are built
mark by mark, evenly and patiently, so that a viewer who lingers discovers that the ornament is
also an inventory. The rigour of a diagram is used to say something tender: that every animal
was counted and every path walked.

**Words as marks, not explanations.** Text is sparse and has two registers only: the elegant
set type of a title, and the loose pencil of a note written on the spot. A label never says
what the image already shows. A few words, placed with painstaking care, anchor the eye and
then step aside.

**Craft is the whole point.** The finished pieces must look meticulously made, with crisp
edges, exact alignments, optical rather than mathematical centring, and nothing crowding the
margins or touching another element. Each one should read as the product of deep expertise
and countless hours of refinement: a museum-grade object that happens to carry a game's name.
